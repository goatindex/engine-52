# Engine 52 — Annotated Bibliography

*The single source of truth for references and precedents across the project. Citation keys in [brackets] are used by the pattern language's Evidence Register and by worked examples. New references enter here first. Maintenance rule: every entry carries (a) full reference, (b) why it matters to this project, (c) which patterns or documents cite it.*

## Method: pattern languages and design formalisms

**[alexander1977]** Alexander, C., Ishikawa, S. & Silverstein, M., *A Pattern Language: Towns, Buildings, Construction* (Oxford UP, 1977).
The form this project adopts: problem-as-forces, conditional discussion, normative therefore, confidence stars, network of links read large-to-small. Read for the *voice* and the linking discipline as much as the content. Cited by: pattern language preface, Appendix B.

**[alexander1979]** Alexander, C., *The Timeless Way of Building* (Oxford UP, 1979).
The theory beneath the form — why patterns are generative rather than catalogued, and what "quality" Alexander thought they produced. Useful for keeping the language a method instead of a taxonomy. Cited by: Appendix B.

**[bjork2005]** Björk, S. & Holopainen, J., *Patterns in Game Design* (Charles River Media, 2005).
The direct ancestor in games: several hundred patterns with explicit instantiates/modulates/conflicts relations — the model for our sympathy/tension mapping. Read before extending the language; check whether a proposed new pattern already exists there under another name. Cited by: Appendix B method.

**[kreimeier2002]** Kreimeier, B., "The Case for Game Design Patterns" (Gamasutra/Game Developer, 2002).
The founding argument for applying Alexander to games; short, worth reading for the objections it anticipates. Cited by: project rationale.

**[church1999]** Church, D., "Formal Abstract Design Tools" (Game Developer, 1999).
Precursor argument for shared design vocabulary; context for why named patterns become community infrastructure (and why renames get expensive — Appendix B rule 4).

**[schell]** Schell, J., *The Art of Game Design: A Book of Lenses* (CRC Press, 3rd ed.).
Patterns in question form. The lens format is a useful complement when a decision resists a normative therefore: a lens interrogates, a pattern instructs.

**[hunicke2004]** Hunicke, R., LeBlanc, M. & Zubek, R., "MDA: A Formal Approach to Game Design and Game Research" (AAAI workshop, 2004).
The mechanics→dynamics→aesthetics chain; the standard vocabulary for explaining why a card-lifecycle choice (mechanics) produces a scarcity arc (dynamics) that lands as desperation or triumph (aesthetics).

**[dormans2012]** Adams, E. & Dormans, J., *Game Mechanics: Advanced Game Design* (New Riders, 2012) — and the Machinations diagram tool.
Internal-economy formalism: sources, drains, converters, feedback loops. The right instrument for stress-testing CARD LIFECYCLE (9) and SHRINKING HANDS (14) arithmetic before any cards are harmed. Cited by: patterns 9, 14.

**[mannsrising2004]** Manns, M.L. & Rising, L., *Fearless Change: Patterns for Introducing New Ideas* (Addison-Wesley, 2004).
Evidence that pattern languages travel beyond architecture and software; a model for community contribution norms around a living language.

## Game design theory and multiplayer structure

**[elias2012]** Elias, G., Garfield, R. & Gutschera, K., *Characteristics of Games* (MIT Press, 2012).
The best treatment of multiplayer politics — kingmaking, bash-the-leader, coalitions — and of comeback/death-spiral structure. Cited by: patterns 4, 6, 14.

**[costikyan2013]** Costikyan, G., *Uncertainty in Games* (MIT Press, 2013).
Taxonomy of uncertainty sources; frames the engine's core swap of randomness for hidden information and physical state.

**[salen2003]** Salen, K. & Zimmerman, E., *Rules of Play* (MIT Press, 2003).
Magic circle and meaningful play; the theoretical ground for why destroying a real object inside a game carries unusual weight.

**[zagal2006]** Zagal, J., Rick, J. & Hsi, I., "Collaborative games: lessons learned from board games," *Simulation & Gaming* 37(1), 2006.
Foundational paper on cooperative design problems including the dominant-player issue. Short and directly applicable. Cited by: patterns 5, 15.

**[engelstein2019]** Engelstein, G. & Shalev, I., *Building Blocks of Tabletop Game Design* (CRC Press, 2nd ed.).
Mechanism encyclopedia; the reference for hand management, drafting, trading, legacy, and semi-coop entries with known failure modes. Cited by: patterns 8, 9, 13, 14, 20.

## Legacy, destruction, and component permanence

**[daviau-gdc]** Daviau, R., GDC talks on legacy design (Risk Legacy postmortem; Board Game Design Day talks).
Primary source on the psychology of permanence and player fear of destruction. Our SESSION AS LIFESPAN (3) compresses his campaign arc; argue with him deliberately. Cited by: patterns 2, 3, 10.

**[leacock-diaries]** Leacock, M., Pandemic Legacy designer diaries.
Practical pacing of permanent change across an arc — the curves to compress into one session. Cited by: pattern 3.

**[exit]** *EXIT: The Game* series (KOSMOS, Brand & Brand).
Commercial proof of single-session component destruction; note how the rules grant explicit permission to destroy. Cited by: patterns 3, 11, 17.

**[tencandles]** Dewey, S., *Ten Candles* (Cavalry Games, 2015).
Destruction as ritual and tone; the GM guidance is the masterclass, not the rules. Cited by: patterns 3, 11, 18.

**[dread]** Ravachol, E., *Dread* (The Impossible Dream, 2005).
Physical component state (the Jenga tower) as shared, visible, escalating game state; what a facilitator does with degradation everyone can see.

**[gloomhaven]** Childres, I., *Gloomhaven* (Cephalofair, 2017).
Sticker-modification logistics at scale; a constrained modification set in practice. Cited by: patterns 10, 16.

**[mysticvale]** *Mystic Vale* (AEG, 2016) and the card-crafting line (*Custom Heroes*, *Edge of Darkness*).
The non-destructive counter-technology: sleeved transparent upgrades, capped modification slots. Keeps our choice of destruction a decision rather than a default. Cited by: patterns 16, 21.

**[fabledfruit]** Friese, F., *Fabled Fruit* (2F-Spiele, 2016).
State persisting between plays without destruction — another point on the persistence spectrum between one-shot and legacy.

**[kingsdilemma]** *The King's Dilemma* (Horrible Guild, 2019).
Sticker-driven legacy plus factional council politics: the best single comp for modification and semi-coop tension together. Cited by: pattern 4.

## Standard-deck precedents

**[quietyear]** Alder, A., *The Quiet Year* (Buried Without Ceremony, 2013).
Structural exploitation of the 52-card anatomy (suits as seasons, deck as clock) and facilitator-light play. Cited by: patterns 2, 7.

**[regicide]** *Regicide* (Badgers from Mars, 2020).
Cooperative play on a standard deck with suit-distinct powers; proof players internalise suit-as-capability instantly. Cited by: patterns 2, 7.

**[castlefalkenstein]** Pondsmith, M., *Castle Falkenstein* (R. Talsorian, 1994).
Cards replacing dice in an RPG; suits mapped to action categories; hand as agency. Cited by: pattern 7.

**[malifaux]** *Malifaux* / *Through the Breach* (Wyrd Games).
The fate deck and "cheating fate" — spending from hand as dramatic timing; the model for jokers as highest authority. Cited by: pattern 23.

**[deadlands]** *Deadlands* (Pinnacle, 1996) and *Savage Worlds*.
Poker hands as resolution and cards as initiative; precedent for set/combo mechanics on a standard deck.

**[thecrew]** Sing, T., *The Crew* (KOSMOS, 2019).
Cooperative trick-taking with distributed tasks under constrained communication — suit-bound hands forcing allocation decisions. Cited by: patterns 7, 15.

**[hanabi]** Bauza, A., *Hanabi* (2010).
The reference design for rules-limited team communication. Cited by: pattern 15.

## Semi-coop, teams, and negotiation

**[deadofwinter]** *Dead of Winter* (Plaid Hat, 2014).
Canonical semi-coop: shared crisis, secret objectives, possible traitor — study its critical reception as much as its rules. Cited by: patterns 4, 5.

**[archipelago]** Boelinger, C., *Archipelago* (Ludically, 2012).
Collective crisis track plus hidden agendas plus possible separatist; the closest analogue to the pandemic-hospitals frame. Cited by: patterns 4, 5.

**[siderealconfluence]** *Sidereal Confluence* (WizKids, 2017).
Simultaneous open negotiation among asymmetric factions; the model for the inter-team trade layer. Cited by: patterns 5, 12.

**[captainsonar]** *Captain Sonar* (Matagot, 2016).
Two teams of four with fixed asymmetric roles; spatial separation as the natural communication constraint. Cited by: pattern 15.

## Megagame and larp

**[wallman]** Wallman, J., and the megagame tradition (*Watch the Skies* materials; megagame design writing, e.g. the "So you want to design a megagame" blog series).
Team structures of ~4–6 with internal roles, control teams, turn cycles, briefing design, goal plurality. The engine aspires to be a portable economy for exactly this form. Cited by: patterns 1, 6, 23, 25.

**[koljonen2019]** Koljonen, J. et al. (eds.), *Larp Design: Creating Role-Play Experiences* (Knutepunkt, 2019).
The best single volume on larp facilitation, props, ritual, and calibration tools; the chapters on physical artefacts map straight onto marked-card mechanics. Cited by: patterns 1, 18, 25.

**[stenros2010]** Stenros, J. & Montola, M. (eds.), *Nordic Larp* (Fëa Livia, 2010).
Context for diegetic objects and embodied mechanics.

## Accessibility

**[heron-mlu]** Heron, M. et al., Meeple Like Us — board game accessibility teardowns and the underlying published research on tabletop accessibility.
The most systematic body of work on colour-blindness, low vision, and physical accessibility in board games; the basis for shape-plus-colour coding and the no-tear substitutions in EVERY HAND PLAYABLE (24). Cited by: pattern 24.

## Real-world precedents

**[tally-indenture]** Split tally sticks (medieval Exchequer practice: stock and foil) and indenture contracts (named for the indented cut separating counterpart copies).
Centuries of real contract law built on the unfakeable physical match of a deliberately divided object — the direct ancestor of TORN HALVES AS CONTRACTS (22). Cited by: pattern 22.
