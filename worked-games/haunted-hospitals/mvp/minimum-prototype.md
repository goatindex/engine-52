# Spirit Ward — Minimum Prototype

*v0.1 — the facilitated-register prototype for the Engine 52 pattern language. Two or three haunted hospitals, one Control, six turns. Built against Pattern Language v0.1.*

---

## What this document is

The second evidence-gathering instrument. Where *Salvage Run* tested the boxed register with faces-only backs and a punch-heavy economy, **Spirit Ward deliberately flips the untested switches**: the facilitated register (Pattern 1), BACKS THAT WHISPER Resolution B (Pattern 19 — completely unstarred), sticker-heavy status play (16, 17), Control-applied marks (18), Control-held jokers (23), and travel at mandated volume (12). The numbers are first guesses, flagged for tuning.

**Register:** facilitated — one player is **Control** and runs the outbreak from a printed script (Pattern 1).
**Players:** 7–13 — two or three hospitals of 3–4, plus Control. **Time:** ~2 hours.

## The story, in one paragraph

A haunting epidemic is sweeping the city. Possession spreads like infection; exorcism is treatment; warding is sanitisation. The hospitals are the public health response — underfunded, rivalrous, and obligated to take each other's overflow. Ghosts mark what they touch, and what a ghost has touched can be recognised even face-down, across a ward, in someone else's hand. If the outbreak track tops out, the city falls and nobody's standing matters.

## Components (Pattern 25)

Per hospital: 1 standard deck (**different back colour per hospital** — Pattern 12), 1 hole punch, stickers, 1 fine marker.
Control: a marker of a distinct colour (Control's ink), the Outbreak Script, a visible outbreak track (paper, 0–20).
*(Accessibility, Pattern 24: square stickers replace punches; a diagonal-cut sticker replaces a tear.)*

## Setup

1. Each hospital takes a deck, removes the jokers, and **hands both jokers to Control** (Pattern 23, megagame resolution: control holds the intervention budget — it scales with hospital count automatically).
2. **Suits as roles (Pattern 7):**

   | Suit | Role | Does |
   |---|---|---|
   | ♥ | **Counsellor** | treats the possessed |
   | ♠ | **Warden** | containment and security |
   | ♣ | **Technician** | wards, gear, prevention |
   | ♦ | **Administrator** | budget — the shield and the shame |

   *Three-player hospital:* the Warden also takes ♣ (Warden-Technician). All hospitals use the same arrangement.
3. **Turn-zero swap (Pattern 8):** bands as standard — face cards to ♠, 2–5 to ♣, 6–10 to ♥, Aces to ♦. Each player gives each teammate one card from that teammate's band.
4. Each player secretly picks one **deniable individual goal** (see Goals).
5. Hospitals sit apart from one another; Control sits where everyone can see the outbreak track (Pattern 15, megagame resolution: spatial separation is the communication constraint).

## Goals (Patterns 4, 5, 6)

- **Shared, dominant, gated:** the **outbreak track** must be below the fall line at the end of turn 6 (**15** for two hospitals, **20** for three — tune). If the city falls, **nobody wins.** The track is public — in this story the shared tier *should* be legible (Pattern 6, soft-setting resolution).
- **Hospital:** highest **Standing** wins (kept on hidden per-hospital columns, revealed at the end).
- **Individual (secret, deniable — Pattern 4's pandemic resolution: friction, not betrayal; each worth 3 Standing):**

  | Role | Choose one |
  |---|---|
  | Counsellor | (C1) Personally cure 4+. (C2) No patient you cured is ever re-possessed. |
  | Warden | (W1) Your hospital never holds the most possessed cards at any Accounting. (W2) Personally commit the top card to 2+ banishments. |
  | Technician | (T1) Ward 4+ times. (T2) No possession ever lands on a rank you warded that turn. |
  | Administrator | (A1) End with 3+ ♦ unspent. (A2) Pay to refuse 2+ mandated transfers. |

## The card lifecycle sentence (Pattern 9)

> **In this game, treating and warding return the card punched; banishing a ghost spends cards forever; transfers send cards — and their ghosts — to other hospitals.**

Unmarked cards are coins; marked cards are objects (Pattern 13).

## The mark grammar (Patterns 16, 17, 18, 19)

| Mark | Name | Applied by | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Sticker** (face) | *possessed* | Control | Status: this card is possessed. Unplayable until cured. Counts against your hospital on the track. |
| **Punch** | *drained* | the player, announced | Permanent: this card has given of itself. Half rank in ward work — **double rank in banishments** (Pattern 20: the rite trusts what has suffered). |
| **Ink on face** (player colour) | *cure-dot* | Control, at cure | Information: this card has been possessed before. |
| **Ink on BACK** (Control's colour) | **ghost sigil** | **Control only** | **Pattern 19, Resolution B: the whisper is the game.** A card possessed a *second* time is permanently haunted: Control sigils its back. It is identifiable face-down, by everyone, forever — even cured, the sigil remains. Haunted cards carry the ghost with them when they travel. |

Routine marks are announce-then-mark; the sigil and the cure-dot are Control-applied — the facilitated register's high-stakes marks (Pattern 18). **Terminal form (Pattern 21):** at its third mark a card becomes a **Vessel** — name it. A Vessel can do nothing except be banished or be committed to a banishment, where it counts double.

## The three systems

### System 1 — The Ward (verb: return, punched) — intra-hospital only

Player actions, two each per Ward Phase:

- **Treat:** play a ♥ of rank ≥ the possessed card's rank. The seal comes off; Control dots the cured card; the played ♥ is **punched and returned** (drained).
- **Ward:** play any ♣ and name a rank. The next possession event cannot land on that rank in your hospital this turn. The ♣ is punched and returned.
- **Banish:** tear one haunted (back-sigiled or Vessel) card **and** commit cards totalling rank **12+ across 2+ suits** (committed cards are punched and returned; drained ♥ count double). The torn card leaves the game. **Outbreak track −2. Standing +3.** Banishment is performed standing, witnessed by Control (Pattern 18: the rite is content).
- **Pass.**

### System 2 — Mutual Aid (verb: travel — at volume, Pattern 12)

During each Conference, in this order:

1. **Mandated surge transfer (Control):** the hospital holding the most possessed cards transfers one possessed card to the hospital holding the fewest. The receiver must accept — **unless its Administrator pays ♦ totalling ≥ the card's rank into the box** (budget as shield: legal, visible, and shameful — the deniable defection this setting wants).
2. **Voluntary trades:** anything moves — cards (marks, dots, sigils and back colours travel with them), Standing transfers (recorded openly), promises.

**The ghost jumps (Patterns 12 + 19):** whenever a back-sigiled card enters a hospital, the receiver immediately seals one card of equal rank in that hospital, if one exists. Contagion travels with the object.

### System 3 — The Outbreak (Control's script; verb: Control applies status)

Each turn, before anything else, Control executes the script line: each hospital must seal the named number of unsealed cards of the named ranks (holders choose which card of a named rank; if a named rank is all sealed/warded/absent, step up one rank).

| Turn | Possessions per hospital | Note |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 × rank 4 | |
| 2 | 1 × rank 4, 1 × rank 8 | |
| 3 | 1 × rank 7, 1 × any face card | **Inject: Poltergeist** — Control gives one hospital a joker as a sealed rank-10 card. It cannot be cured, only banished. |
| 4 | 3 × ranks 3, 6, 9 | |
| 5 | 2 × any, 1 × any Ace | **Inject: the Medium** — the hospital with fewest possessed cards receives the second joker: a wildcard counting rank 10 toward any banishment. |
| 6 | 2 × any | |

**Second possession = haunting:** any card carrying a cure-dot that is sealed again gets the **ghost sigil** on its back from Control.

**Accounting (end of each turn):** outbreak track rises **+1 per possessed card citywide**. The track is public and read aloud.

## Turn structure (×6)

1. **Outbreak** — Control runs the script.
2. **Conference (4 min)** — open, cross-hospital: mandated transfer, then free negotiation. Anyone may speak to anyone (Pattern 15: the fiction and the room constrain, not the rules).
3. **Ward Phase** — hospitals work privately, two actions per player. No cross-hospital talk.
4. **Accounting** — track update, read aloud.

## End and scoring

City survives → each hospital totals Standing: **+1 per cure, +3 per banishment, +3 per met individual goal, −2 to the hospital holding the most possessed cards at the end.** Highest Standing wins; Control narrates the commendations. City falls → debrief anyway; the data is the score.

## Budget arithmetic (Pattern 9 check)

Possession script seals ~9–10 cards per hospital across the session; cures recycle them at a cost of one punched ♥ each; banishments spend 1 + drain ~2–3. Hands stay near 11–13 with rising mark density — ruin as *texture*, not shrinkage (contrast both other prototypes). If the track outruns cure capacity before turn 4, soften the script before touching the thresholds.

## What this prototype tests

| Hypothesis under test | Pattern | What to watch |
|---|---|---|
| **Resolution B is a game, not a bug** — back-sigils create a tracking/dread layer worth the loss of anonymity | 19 | Did players track sigiled cards across transfers? Did anyone refuse a trade on sight of a back? |
| Contagion-through-travel is parseable and fun | 12 + 19 | Ghost-jump moments: dramatic, or bookkeeping? |
| Mandated travel at volume erodes role identity | 12 vs 7 | By turn 6, did hands still read as roles? |
| Control-applied marks give the facilitator a runtime presence without slowing play | 18 | Queue time waiting on Control's marker |
| Sticker-heavy status play parses at a glance | 16, 17 | Time reading state vs deciding |
| A dominant gated shared tier with deniable individual goals produces friction, not betrayal | 4, 5 | Was defection (paying to refuse transfers, hoarding ♦) felt as *characterful* or as griefing? |
| Public shared track + hidden Standing keeps competition civil but real | 6 | Any bash-the-leader behaviour despite hidden Standing? |
| Control's joker injects land as drama, not punishment | 23 | Table reaction at turns 3 and 5 |
| Drained-double banishment makes ruin desirable | 20 | Did anyone treat *in order to* drain hearts for the rite? |
| The script makes Control runnable by a first-timer | 1, 25 | Control's rules questions per turn |
